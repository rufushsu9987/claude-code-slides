# Brand-neutral visual quality

Use this reference for every deck, regardless of theme. Treat a selected template or supplied brand system as an override for tokens and motifs, not for communication quality, accessibility, or evidence integrity.

## Visual objective

Make the intended audience understand the page's conclusion and supporting evidence within a few seconds. Every visual choice must serve hierarchy, meaning, navigation, or comprehension.

## Hierarchy and composition

- Give each page one dominant claim or visual anchor.
- Use claim-style headlines and make supporting evidence visibly subordinate.
- Establish an intentional eye path through scale, position, contrast, and whitespace.
- Align elements to a clear grid; remove accidental near-alignments.
- Prefer fewer, larger elements over dense collections of equally weighted objects.
- Keep critical content inside safe areas and verify it at presentation and thumbnail size.

## Typography and density

- Use the supplied brand fonts when available and licensed; otherwise choose portable system stacks.
- Keep body text, labels, code, sources, and chart annotations legible from the back of the room.
- Limit line length and avoid shrinking type to preserve excess copy.
- Move nuance, caveats, and secondary evidence into notes when the audience does not need them on the canvas.
- Use monospace only when it communicates code, commands, identifiers, measurements, or metadata.

## Color and accessibility

- Maintain WCAG AA contrast for essential text and controls.
- Do not rely on color alone for status, categories, or paths; add labels, shapes, patterns, or position.
- Use accent colors to establish meaning and priority, not to decorate every object.
- Provide useful alt text or a nearby text equivalent for meaningful images and diagrams.
- Preserve visible keyboard focus, semantic reading order, and reduced-motion behavior in interactive formats.
- Check diagrams and charts in grayscale and under common color-vision deficiencies.

## Evidence, diagrams, and data

- Choose the medium from the communication job: real artifact, screenshot, verified chart, native editable shapes, custom SVG, or whitespace.
- Direct-label important nodes and series; label arrows with the relationship, protocol, state change, or ownership they represent.
- Use truthful scales, baselines, units, dates, and source notes.
- Distinguish verified facts from assumptions and proposed future states.
- Show boundaries only when they represent real trust, network, tenancy, ownership, or responsibility boundaries.
- Use loops only when feedback changes a later cycle; use hubs only when the connections carry meaning.
- Remove generic filler diagrams, ornamental dashboards, and repeated cards that do not explain or prove the headline.

## Layout rhythm

- Select layouts by semantic role, then vary scale, density, background, and dominant geometry to support the story.
- Avoid exact consecutive layout repetition unless the pages form a deliberate matched comparison.
- Alternate editorial claims with evidence, sequences, architectures, comparisons, risks, and decisions where the narrative supports them.
- Use whitespace as an active structural element; an intentionally empty region is better than a weak visual.

## Motion and delivery

- Use motion to reveal hierarchy or causality, not to animate every object.
- Keep the page fully understandable without animation.
- Verify the actual delivery surface: browser viewport and print, Marp export, or generated PowerPoint.
- Check local assets, overflow, font fallback, speaker notes, and projection-distance readability before handoff.

## Final visual audit

Confirm that:

- the headline, visual, and evidence communicate the same conclusion
- one element clearly leads and no decoration competes with it
- every label, value, date, unit, and source is accurate
- contrast, type size, focus, reading order, and text alternatives are usable
- the selected theme is applied consistently without overriding the user's brand
- the page remains clear at full size, thumbnail size, in grayscale, and in its exported format
